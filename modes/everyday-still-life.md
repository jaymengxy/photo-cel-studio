# everyday-still-life

## When to use
Household object studies, small objects, fish in a bucket, kitchen utensils and casual still-life arrangements without a more specific photo mode.

## Source cues
isolated items, container rims, reflective surfaces, small animal/fish presence, object grouping.

## Composition strategy
Keep relative object positions and camera view; transform container geometry and interior water into clean readable graphic shapes.

## Linework
Decisive object rims and a few meaningful interior details; fine thin contours for fish fins and animal anatomy.

## Palette
Use the original material's flat base tones with small identity-defining accent colors.

## Shadow grammar
One hard-edge body shadow per material, restrained highlights for reflective water/plastic.

## Background policy
Simplify incidental grit and background without removing necessary container structure.

## Preservation guardrails
Fish and animal count when determinable, species/body shapes, object count, relative position, container/prop identity.

## Negative constraints
No extra fish, changed species, additional pets, imaginary contents or cute mascot faces.

## Quality checks
Object/fish/animal anatomy and count remain as observed; clearly cel-drawn not generic icons.
