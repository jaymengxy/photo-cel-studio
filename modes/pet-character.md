# pet-character

## When to use
Domestic animals such as cats, dogs, rabbits, birds and their portrait or interaction moments.

## Source cues
one or more recognizable pets, breed-specific face, distinctive coat markings, ears, eyes, pose.

## Composition strategy
Place the actual pet pose center stage; protect paws, gaze direction, fur silhouette and environmental contact.

## Linework
Fluid animal-specific anatomy and expressive ear/eye contours, selective fur clusters, no traced individual hairs.

## Palette
Keep original coat/hair markings and tonal separation with 2–3 clear cel color areas.

## Shadow grammar
Rounded but hard-bound shading masses appropriate to observed coat volume and light source.

## Background policy
Reduce clutter around pet; protect bed, carrier, human or play-object relationships.

## Preservation guardrails
Breed/species cues, coat markings, limb/tail count, ear and eye configuration, body size and action.

## Negative constraints
No automatic anthropomorphism, human clothes, exaggerated kawaii eyes, extra paws or changed species.

## Quality checks
Owner can recognize the specific pet rather than a generic animated animal.
