# quiet-dramatic

## When to use
Still gestures, waiting, quiet contemplation, seated person, solitary street portrait or unhurried object interaction.

## Source cues
one hero, reflective posture, broad negative space, calm eye line or downcast gaze.

## Composition strategy
Preserve stillness; use original empty wall/bench/doorway as a graphic field and let small objects contribute to narrative.

## Linework
Economical facial/body contours; carefully describe shoulders, eyewear, fingers, garments and objects.

## Palette
Restrained hues extracted from garments and setting; one source-grounded chromatic accent.

## Shadow grammar
Bold but simple quiet shadow planes, gentle edge control and modest highlights.

## Background policy
Reduce noise, not story: keep bench geometry, thermos, bag, hat and major location cues when present.

## Preservation guardrails
Exact observed seated stance, hands and props, expression, age and gesture of the individual.

## Negative constraints
No dramatic new emotion, heroic pose, fashion-beautified face, added people or glowing eyes.

## Quality checks
Mood is communicated through framing and light, not changes to identity or event.
