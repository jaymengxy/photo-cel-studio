# vehicle-mechanical

## When to use
Cars, motorcycles, bicycles, trains, trams and transport as primary photographic subject.

Default pairing: **mature-ova**, including city motorcycle photos. Recommend **industrial-mecha-cel** as a separate mechanical comparison; switch only on an explicit user choice or authorized `cel_style_profile: auto`, never because this Scene Mode was auto-selected. Explicit clean-modern or another registered profile remains selected.

## Source cues
wheel spacing, body panels, chassis, windows, lights, identifiable mechanical interfaces.

## Composition strategy
Maintain exact camera azimuth, vehicle proportion and wheel/contact geometry; graphic crop permissible only with user intent.

## Linework
Mechanical contour fidelity, crisp negative-space openings, minimal but exact design seams.

Weight the load-bearing silhouette, lighten internal construction and background lines within the selected profile. Check motorcycle **wheel geometry**, **front fork**, **handlebar**, **engine block**, **frame structure**, **headlight**, **suspension** and **mechanical perspective** against visible source parts. Wheel ellipses, steering/fork axes and panel perspective must agree; preserve correct axle/fork/engine/frame connections and rider hand-to-grip contact. Do not invent hidden components.

## Palette
Use actual paint color and restrained hard-edge enamel/reflection color blocks.

For mature-ova daylight, retain yellow rider clothing and orange/white truck identity accents when present, while quieting large sky, road and building planes. Keep daytime and observed light color; avoid sparkling tourism-poster brightness. An explicit clean-modern comparison can use clearer secondary colors within the same source identity/light constraints.

## Shadow grammar
Hard planar underbody, cabin and window reflections coherent with original surrounding lights.

Use Base / Shadow / optional Highlight as 2–3 principal values to model curved tires, helmet, rider fabric and solid metal volumes. Reflections are sparse flat geometric patches; no smooth PBR chrome or shadows added merely to look cinematic.

## Background policy
Render roads/rails and buildings as cel backgrounds; do not displace wheels relative to ground.

Under mature-ova, use traditionally painted wall/asphalt/foliage masses with lighter distant ink. Keep street depth, traffic signals, palms, road markings and regional architectural cues when visible, plus the rider/truck/car spatial order. Simplify texture without making the city an idealized travel advertisement; clean-modern follows its cleaner paint-edge policy instead.

Apply the fixed mature method in `references/mature-cel-render.md`: stronger pressure-varied outer ink, sparse interior folds/seams, opaque rider base plus one connected shadow, selective flat chrome strokes and coherent engine masses. Quiet roads/sky/wall shadows into supported gray-blue painted planes while source sunny planes remain daylight and yellow/orange identity accents survive. Do not simplify away fork/axle/grip/frame connections. Keep native plus verified original-ratio long-edge-540 delivery unless overridden.

## Preservation guardrails
Wheel count, model-recognizable silhouette, mirrors, handlebars, door/window pattern, perspective and key components.

## Negative constraints
No car model substitution, spoilers, futuristic conversion, cloned wheels, invented badges or motion.

No new mecha, robots, futuristic technology or vehicles absent from the source. Avoid uniform photo-edge tracing, plastic bodywork, gradient metal shine and unrequested tourism-poster styling.

## Quality checks
Same physical vehicle is recognizable with correct geometry.

Check all visible motorcycle components and ground contacts, rider grip/pose, vehicle count and background relationships independently of style. Then check selected-profile contour hierarchy, volume-building hard shadows and drawn city background. Mechanical accuracy alone does not pass the Style Authenticity Gate.
