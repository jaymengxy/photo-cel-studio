# industrial-mecha-cel

## Profile ID
`industrial-mecha-cel` — mechanical drawing language, not a fictional-machine conversion.

## When to use
Explicit industrial/mechanical cel treatment or authorized `cel_style_profile: auto` with dominant vehicle/machine construction. Merely recognizing a car/motorcycle under the default does not select this profile; recommend a separate comparison while retaining mature-ova.

## Visual intent
Weighty traditionally drawn transport/industrial volumes, precise mechanical relationships, deliberate internal construction and angular metal shade plates. Keep the actual machine, scale and engineering; industrial weight is drawn form, not extra armor.

## Linework
Weight load-bearing outer forms, tire profiles and body shells; use thinner exact seams, fasteners and structural openings. Deliberately vary pressure on curved panels while keeping straight structural joins accurate. Do not invent bolts to simulate detail or outline every photographic reflection.

## Color palette
Group metal, rubber, paint and glass into restrained source-derived gray/blue/earth planes with distinct identity paint accents. Keep yellow clothing and orange/white trucks recognizable when present. Do not recolor an ordinary vehicle to tactical charcoal or futuristic neon.

## Shadow grammar
Use 2–3 principal values with hard angular underbody, engine, panel and structural-recess shadows aligned to observed illumination. Flat metal highlights describe curvature and plane changes; a few clear reflection patches replace complex photo specular gradients. Avoid modern 3D/PBR chrome, ray-traced sheen and glowing edge lights.

## Background painting
Paint roads, transport infrastructure and surrounding buildings as credible planar layers. Preserve actual lane markings, rail alignment, curb contacts, street depth and scale; distinguish background architecture from foreground mechanical ink. No fantasy hangar or cyberpunk skyline.

## Character and object treatment
Check visible components and their relationships individually; hidden parts stay obscured:
- **Motorcycle wheel geometry:** correct count, ellipse axes, diameter, wheelbase, tire thickness and road contacts at the source viewing angle.
- **Front fork:** two legs where visible, correct rake and connection from steering head to front axle; no disconnected fork/axle.
- **Handlebar:** original reach, grips/mirrors and rider hand contact, aligned with steering and fork.
- **Engine block:** observed position, scale and simplified cylinder/case planes; preserve negative space around it.
- **Frame structure:** real connecting members and joints around engine/seat; no floating tubes or extra chassis.
- **Headlight:** correct shape, position, mount and light state, not a futuristic beacon.
- **Suspension:** retain visible spring/shock placement and physical attachment, never add unseen systems.
- **Mechanical perspective:** wheels, fork, handlebar, frame and panels agree with the same camera, depth and steering direction.
People/animals keep biological proportions and natural expressions, not robotic styling.

## Material / analog finish
Matte drawn enamel, graphic glass/rubber separation and subtle cel/paint variation. Fine optional grain stays below seams and wheel geometry; industrial texture does not mean dirt/rust invented on a clean source. Texture never supplies missing engineering detail.

## Compatible Scene Modes
Vehicle-mechanical, sci-fi-industrial and architecture-graphic are strong pairings. All other modes can use the style while keeping their own source-specific anatomy and object rules.

## Atmosphere compatibility
All six with evidence. Wet/neon reflection patches must follow actual surfaces and lamps; snow must rest on observed parts, mist must not erase foreground mechanics. Original daytime light remains daytime.

## Preservation guardrails
P0/P1 identity, rider pose, count, make-recognizable geometry and subject/background relations outrank visual weight. Preserve **light direction** and source hue accents. **Master-lock** retains accepted components and finish outside the narrow edit.

## Negative constraints
No robots, science-fiction mecha, weapons, added armor, futuristic tech, copied fictional machines, extra wheels or engine components. No vehicle substitution, fake badges, disconnected forks, altered rider grip or PBR/3D shiny materials.

## Quality checks
All visible eight motorcycle component checks pass; wheels contact the actual ground and rider hands contact the actual grips. Ink and hard metal planes emphasize engineering more than mature-ova without changing the machine. Background stays drawn and credible; metal reflections are graphic and restrained.
