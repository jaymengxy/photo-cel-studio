# urban-noir-cel

## Profile ID
`urban-noir-cel` — urban tonal treatment, not a weather/time setting.

## When to use
Explicit cool, reserved urban-film treatment; authorized auto for urban photos already showing cool/deep value groups and strong observed shadow contrast. Noir can apply to a sunny street: **daytime stays daytime**.

## Visual intent
Mature, restrained, solitary urban cinematic drawing carried by existing human/architectural relationships, deeper secondary value groups and crisp light/shadow divisions. Convey mood through drawing hierarchy without changing expression, staging or event.

## Linework
Slightly heavier silhouettes than mature-ova, sparse fine interior detail in open light and selective contour merges inside already dark masses. Retain separating lines for overlapping hands, faces and machinery even when nearby shadows merge. Never turn a recognizable subject into an unreadable black icon.

## Color palette
Bias secondary road, wall and distant building planes toward subdued cool blue-gray, charcoal or muted violet while retaining original material hues and identity accents. Darken only within the observed value hierarchy. Warm sun/lamps and skin remain source-grounded; noir does not force every light blue or lower the sky into a night exposure.

## Shadow grammar
Use 2–3 flat base/shadow/highlight values with stronger value separation than mature-ova where real illumination supports it. Let existing recesses join broader dark groups without adding cast-shadow shapes. Preserve light direction, contact shadows and lit-side facial legibility.

## Background painting
Treat actual façades, alleys, crossings and street depths as large painted value groups, with lighter detail at distance. Keep vanishing points, cultural signage shapes and people's relative positions; original negative space carries isolation without deleting bystanders or inventing a noir alley.

## Character and object treatment
Authentic age, expression, anatomy, clothes and vehicle silhouettes remain intact. Do not impose sadness, cigarettes, weapons, a detective costume or a new narrative on a candid subject.

## Material / analog finish
Subtle matte cel/paint variation and fine film-like texture; no heavy vignette, crushed black filter, gritty grime or dramatic scratches. Surfaces should read as drawn plates before texture is visible.

## Compatible Scene Modes
Urban-cinematic, quiet-dramatic, portrait-character, architecture-graphic and interior-atmosphere are strong pairings. All other registered modes remain usable; noir tone never substitutes for their geometry/acting rules.

## Atmosphere compatibility
All six when observed. A dry daylight photo gets none unless it shows another supported condition. Keep real golden-hour warmth, snow locations or backlighting; neon-night + rainy requires actual night light and wet surfaces. No automatic conversion to rain, neon or night.

## Preservation guardrails
P0/P1 identity, event, count and place structure outrank dark merges or cool palette. Keep **light direction**, original time/weather and identity colors. **Master-lock** freezes the accepted tone and all regions outside the requested edit.

## Negative constraints
No invented night, rain, neon signs, wet roads, futuristic architecture, new props or false emotion. Avoid cheerful tourism-poster treatment, ornamental glow, crushed-face shadows and glossy digital/PBR materials. Do not confuse noir with compulsory nighttime.

## Quality checks
Existing city relationships read more reserved through cool secondary planes and clear source-motivated contrast; heavier line hierarchy and selective dark merges remain legible. Daytime stays daytime and dry roads stay dry. Compare against mature-ova for tonal emphasis without changed scene facts.
